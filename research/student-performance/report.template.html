Predicting school grades with open data

Research only: adding age and educational context did not beat repeating the last grade (G2) in mean absolute error. These probabilities must not be used to assess Aule children or promise improvement.

Interpretation and limits

G2 carries most of the predictive signal. Age did not add a clear improvement in these secondary-school data; this does not establish its relevance for primary-school activities or language. Educational support can be received because a pupil is struggling, so its coefficient is not the effect of providing help.

Only 18 mathematics and 56 Portuguese records gained at least two points. The extended model overestimated the frequency of improvement. Probability calibration and individual decisions require prospective evidence.

The data cover two Portuguese schools, ages 15–22: 395 mathematics and 649 Portuguese records, with 382 pupils overlapping. Subjects are evaluated separately. Questionnaire timing is unverified; G3 may incorporate earlier performance. No translation to C/B/A/AD is justified. No Aule records were used.

The dataset lacks skill-level attempts, hints, retention probes, tutor interventions and competency rubrics. It cannot test those factors or explain what causes a child to improve. All zero grades remain in the evaluation; sensitive and potentially future-derived predictors are excluded.

Before operational use, collect consented, time-stamped assessments with teacher-reviewed rubrics; hold out later periods and pupils; evaluate calibration and age/grade slices; compare with the last observed grade. Causal recommendations need a separate appropriate study design.
